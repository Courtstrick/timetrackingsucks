    A short, angry manifesto.

    Let's say the quiet part out loud: time tracking is a tax on doing the actual job.

    You did the work. You know you did it. Your manager knows you did it. And still, every Friday, you sit there reconstructing forty hours of your one wild and precious life from calendar invites and vibes — rounding a ninety-minute meeting you'd pay to forget into a tidy "1.5 — Stakeholder Sync."

    That's not tracking. That's historical fiction with a submit button.

    Here's a secret from inside the building: everyone knows. Finance knows the numbers are soft. Entire quarterly forecasts get built on data invented in a panic by someone who just wanted to log off. The timesheet doesn't measure your work. It measures how good you are at remembering — or inventing — what you did. Those are different skills. Neither one ships anything.

    We're not here to fix it. Smarter, richer people have tried and failed. We're here to admit it — loudly, together, and with receipts.
